Notch Orchestrator

See which session
needs you.

A small macOS app that lives in the MacBook notch and keeps an eye on your Claude Code sessions, in the terminal and in the desktop app alike.

How to read the island.

Every state has its own colour and its own shape, so it never rests on colour alone. Counters are numbers beside a mark, not one dot per session.

State

Usage ring

The ring is your five-hour limit, and stays quiet until the limit comes close. The weekly limit is in the list and in the menu.

Answer from the notch

Allow or deny a tool call, or pick an answer to the agent's question, without switching windows. A request you ignore goes back to Claude Code's own dialog after five minutes.

Jump to the session

One click takes you to the exact Terminal tab, the session in the Claude desktop app, or the VS Code window.

CI of what a session pushed

Once a session pushes, its row shows the pipeline's state and stage, on GitHub and GitLab, through your own gh and glab sign-in.

Without the mouse.

A hotkey opens the list of sessions from any app. ⌃⌥Y allows the request on the card, ⌃⌥X denies it.

Opens the list. Press it right now, or tap the keys.

It interrupts as much as you let it

Loud
Expands and sounds for every request and every finished turn.
Smart
Only for a session that is not already in front of you.
Quiet
Counters only.

A Focus silences every mode.

How it knows.

Hooks, then a second look

On first launch the app adds its hooks to ~/.claude/settings.json, keeps a backup of the file, and wraps your status line so that usage limits reach it. What the hooks say is checked against the session's process and transcript, and what cannot be confirmed shows as unknown.

Nothing leaves your Mac

No telemetry and no backend. The app holds no tokens. All that goes out is its own check for a newer release, and what gh and glab ask your git host about a pipeline.

What it does not do

  • Sessions started before the app was installed do not report to it until they are restarted.
  • The app is not notarized by Apple. A centrally managed Mac may refuse it.
  • Without Full Disk Access it cannot see a Focus, so it does not respect one.
  • Usage of desktop-only sessions comes without reset times.
  • A git host the CLI is not signed in to shows as "no access".

Try it on your Mac.

Paste this into a terminal.

curl -fsSL https://github.com/yegoshua/notch-orchestrator/releases/latest/download/install.sh | sh

macOS 14 or later · MIT

  1. 1

    The installer downloads the latest release, puts the app into Applications and starts it. macOS accepts an app that curl downloaded, and refuses the same one from a browser, which is why this is the way in.

  2. 2

    The app connects itself to Claude Code. Restart the sessions that were already running.

  3. 3

    macOS asks to automate Terminal when that is first needed: to jump to a session's tab and to tell whether the tab is in front. "Start at Login" is in the menu bar menu.

With Homebrew: brew install --cask yegoshua/tap/notch-orchestrator
